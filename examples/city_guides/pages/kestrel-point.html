Kestrel Point Tide Pools: A Low-Tide Timetable and What You Will Find

Kestrel Point is only worth visiting for the rock shelf if you arrive within an hour of low tide. At high water the pools are under two metres of surf and the stairs at the north end are closed.

The shelf holds ochre sea stars, giant green anemones, and in late summer the occasional octopus. Rangers lead free walks from the north stairs on the lowest tides of each month.

Wear shoes with grip; the green algae on the lower shelf is the reason for most of the rescues each year. Collecting anything, including empty shells, carries a fine.

The fish shack at the harbour mouth fries whatever the two local boats landed that morning and closes when it sells out, usually by 3pm.
